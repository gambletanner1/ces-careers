Childersburg Elementary is a Pre-K through 5th grade school of about 280 students in Talladega County Schools. We’re looking for teachers and staff who want to keep getting better, and this page shows exactly how we’ll help you do it.

See open positions How we support new teachers

A small school where your work gets noticed

With around fifteen classroom teachers, you won’t be a name on a roster here. You’ll know students across every grade, plan alongside a team that shares your hallway, and see your ideas turn into schoolwide practice.

We hire Pre-K and K–5 classroom teachers, special education and intervention teachers, instructional assistants, and support staff in child nutrition, custodial, and office roles. Openings change each year, and every current one is posted by the district.

Who thrives at CES

  • You believe every child can learn to read, write, and reason, and you plan like it.
  • You want honest feedback, and you give it kindly.
  • You’d rather solve a problem with a colleague than alone.
  • You see families as partners in their child’s learning.

Your path at Childersburg Elementary

From the day you apply to the year you start mentoring others, here’s what to expect at each step.

1

Apply through the district

Every teaching and support position at Childersburg Elementary is posted by Talladega County Schools and filled through the state’s HireTrue application system.

How to apply

  1. Browse current openings on the Talladega County Schools website.
  2. Create a free account in HireTrue, the application system used by the Alabama State Department of Education.
  3. Complete your profile and attach the documents the posting asks for.
  4. Apply to the Childersburg Elementary posting that fits you.
Current openings Apply on HireTrue

Questions before you apply?

For help with the application itself, call the district personnel office at (256) 315-5136.

To ask about a position or schedule a visit, call the school office at (256) 315-5525. We’re glad to show you around before you decide.

2

Interview with the people you’ll work with

We hire as a team, so you meet your future colleagues, and they meet you, before anyone makes a decision.

What happens after you apply

  1. Screening. We review each application against the posting’s requirements and Alabama certification.
  2. Team interview. You’ll meet the principal, a grade-level teacher, and a specialist such as a reading coach or special education teacher.
  3. A short teaching sample. Teach a 10-minute reading or math mini-lesson, or talk us through how you’d handle a real classroom scenario. You choose.
  4. References and background check. Alabama requires fingerprint-based background clearance for school employees.
  5. Recommendation. The principal recommends the selected candidate to the superintendent, who brings it to the Board of Education.

How we decide

Every candidate for a position answers the same core questions and is scored on the same rubric:

  • Knowledge of how young children learn to read and do math
  • Planning lessons that reach every learner
  • Building a warm, well-run classroom
  • Using student work and data to adjust teaching
  • Working well with colleagues and families

We let every candidate know the outcome, whether or not you’re selected.

3

Your first two years: a mentor and a cohort

Through the Alabama Teacher Mentor Program, first- and second-year teachers are paired with a mentor who has at least three years of successful teaching experience. At CES, we build more support around that.

Before students arrive

  • New-teacher orientation on school routines, curriculum, technology, and who to ask for what
  • A classroom setup day with your mentor and grade-level team

Experienced, but new to us?

Teachers who join CES with experience are paired with a building partner for their first semester, so you learn how things work here without starting over.

During the year

  • Weekly mentor check-ins for the first nine weeks, then every other week
  • Release time to watch colleagues teach, at least twice each semester
  • A monthly new-teacher cohort meeting with the principal, built around your questions
  • A midyear conversation about what’s working and what support you still need
4

Feedback that helps you grow

Evaluation here is a year-long conversation about your teaching, grounded in Alabama’s teaching standards and evidence from your classroom.

  1. Fall

    Set goals

    Reflect on your practice and set one or two professional goals with an administrator.

  2. All year

    Frequent, short visits

    Brief walkthroughs plus formal observations, with written feedback within two school days.

  3. Winter

    Midyear check-in

    Look at student progress and your goals together, and adjust your support.

  4. Spring

    Reflect and plan

    A summative conference that names your strengths, one focus for next year, and the support you’ll get.

Every piece of feedback points to specific evidence and ends with a next step, plus a resource or a colleague who can help you take it.

5

Keep learning, and lead when you’re ready

Professional learning at CES is built around what you teach and where you want to grow, not a one-size-fits-all agenda.

Learning that fits you

  • Weekly grade-level PLCs to plan together and study student work
  • State-supported training in literacy through the Alabama Reading Initiative, and in math and science through AMSTI
  • Choice-based sessions on in-service days, matched to your goals
  • Support for National Board Certification, added certifications, and advanced degrees, which also move you up the state salary schedule

Ways to lead

  • Mentor a new teacher
  • Facilitate your grade-level PLC
  • Share a strategy at a faculty meeting
  • Serve on the leadership, wellness, or school improvement team

Our leaders learn too

Each year our administrators share their own growth goals with staff, ask for anonymous feedback on their leadership, and report back on what they’re changing because of it.

How we treat each other

Teaching is hard to sustain alone. These are the commitments our staff makes to one another.

  • Protected planning time

    Grade-level teams share a planning period, and we keep it free of administrative meetings.

  • Meetings worth attending

    Faculty meetings have a posted agenda and end on time. If something can be an email, it’s an email.

  • Evenings are yours

    No one is expected to answer school email at night or on weekends.

  • Help without apology

    Need a minute, or a hand with a tough moment? Call for backup, and a colleague or administrator steps in.

  • Celebrate out loud

    Staff shout-outs open every faculty meeting, and anyone can post a thank-you on the staff board.

  • Your voice shapes the school

    A staff climate survey each semester, with the results and our response shared at the next faculty meeting.

Benefits that take care of you

As a Talladega County Schools employee, you receive Alabama’s public education benefits, plus the support that comes with working at CES.

BenefitWhat it means for youProvided through
Salary Pay follows the Alabama state salary schedule and rises with your years of experience and your degree level. State of Alabama and Talladega County Schools
Health coverage Medical coverage for you and your family, with optional cancer, dental, hospital indemnity, and vision plans. PEEHIP, the Public Education Employees’ Health Insurance Plan
Retirement A pension that pays a monthly benefit for life after you retire, building with every year of service. Teachers’ Retirement System of Alabama
Extra savings Optional tax-advantaged retirement savings on top of your pension. RSA-1 Deferred Compensation Plan
Paid leave Sick leave that builds every month you work, plus personal leave days. Alabama law and district policy
Mentor pay A stipend when you serve as a mentor to a first- or second-year teacher. Alabama Teacher Mentor Program
Professional learning State and district training at no cost to you, scheduled during contract time whenever possible. Alabama State Department of Education and Talladega County Schools
Calendar A school-year schedule with summer, winter, and spring breaks. Talladega County Schools calendar

This is a summary. Plan details, eligibility, and costs are set by PEEHIP, the Retirement Systems of Alabama, and Talladega County Schools, and can change. For current specifics, call the district personnel office at (256) 315-5136.
